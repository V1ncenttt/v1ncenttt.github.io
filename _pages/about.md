Bonjour, I'm Vincent — a French-grown, London-based computer science enthusiast currently deep-diving into Artificial Intelligence at Imperial College London. I spend most of my time either training neural networks or being trained by them (emotionally speaking). My interests lie somewhere between equations, climate models, and why my GPU still isn’t fast enough.

When I’m not trying to make machines learn, I enjoy pondering big questions like: “Can AI fix the weather?” or “What if a transformer learned to predict the apocalypse?” I thrive on interdisciplinary chaos, where math meets code, and code meets reality. Whether it’s diagnosing Alzheimer’s, simulating fluid dynamics, or taming language models, I enjoy turning data into decisions — ideally ones that don’t crash.

Education  
======

I’ve had the joy (and caffeine consumption) of studying in three top institutions across Europe.

- **Imperial College London**  
  *MSc Computing – Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning* (2024–2025)  
  Here I study how to make machines think smarter, faster, and (ideally) with fewer hallucinations. Courses include everything from Graph Neural Networks to Computational Neurodynamics — a.k.a. “why your brain and your GPU aren’t that different.”

- **CentraleSupélec**  
  *Engineering Diploma, Applied Mathematics* (2023–2024)  
  This was where I bonded with partial differential equations, survival analysis, and the cruel elegance of measure theory. I also learned that French espresso pairs beautifully with statistical convergence.

- **University College London (UCL)**  
  *BSc Computer Science, Minor in Intelligent Systems* (2020–2023)  
  I graduated with First Class Honours and probably First Class Debugging Fatigue. This is where I learned to love compilers, robots, and the joy of passing tests on the first try (rare, but glorious).

Research Interests  
======

My brain is happiest when it's solving hard, messy problems — the kind that involve physics, biology, or clouds (of both the atmospheric and computational variety).

I’ve explored generative and deterministic models for global weather forecasting, played with medical images to spot early signs of Alzheimer’s, and even tried to teach Large Language Models to behave (they didn’t). I enjoy applying machine learning to domains that matter, where stakes are high and datasets are wild.

But I also appreciate the elegance of theory — loss bounds, VC dimensions, and those lovely theorems that only occasionally make me cry.

When I'm not busy writing code or LaTeX equations, you'll likely find me sprinting down a football pitch (captain duties included), hanging from a bouldering wall, or jogging around London trying to convince myself it's fun.

I've also dabbled in Model United Nations, because nothing says “teamwork” like negotiating global peace with strangers in suits.
